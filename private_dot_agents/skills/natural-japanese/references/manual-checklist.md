# Manual review checklist

Use this checklist when tools are unavailable or when a direct review is sufficient. It covers the same kinds of reader-facing concerns as the optional tools, but does not reproduce their statistics, classify authorship, or require a clean lint result. The checks are editorial heuristics informed by [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Preserve source meaning

Before stylistic edits, check facts, names, numbers, dates, units, attribution, actors, certainty, obligations, permissions, conditions, negation, exceptions, quantifiers, and causal claims. Compare source and draft after any split, deletion, or heading rewrite.

Do not turn 「次回相談する」 into agreement, 「予定」 into a commitment, or 「一部」 into a majority. Do not invent missing owners, deadlines, numbers, or personal opinions. See `examples.md`.

## 2. Locate the reader's answer

Read headings and paragraph openings together. Can the intended reader find the result, decision, request, procedure, or unresolved question? Does the order match the task? A narrative can reveal its point late; a reference can use stable label headings. Do not force all documents into a summary-first argument.

## 3. Check sentence relations

For difficult sentences, identify who does what, which modifier attaches to which word, and what each connector means. Split a sentence when this clarifies the relations, preserving conditions and qualifiers. Long sentences are review candidates, not automatic failures.

The 2022 public-writing commentary suggests checking readability around 50–60 characters; it explicitly rejects a universal appropriate length. Do not create a short/long alternation or target sentence-length variance.

## 4. Inspect negation and conditions

Check nested negatives and exclusions carefully. Do not collapse 「できないわけではない」 into an unconditional 「できる」 when it carries reluctance or limited possibility. Check 「すべて」「一部」「だけ」「以外」「なら」「限り」 and the scope of each condition.

If the meaning is ambiguous, identify the ambiguity rather than silently choosing a reading. Preserve obligation expressions such as 「しなければならない」.

## 5. Review wording in context

Use `forbidden-patterns.md` and `translationese.md` for alternatives, not as ban lists. A phrase such as 「重要なのは」「このように」「することができます」 may be useful. Shorten only when the same meaning, courtesy, emphasis, and reader support remain.

Do not determine a defect by counting three matches or assigning a fixed phrase density.

## 6. Examine repetition without forcing variety

Ask whether repeated openings, terms, endings, or paragraph patterns help comparison and retrieval or merely restate the same content. Preserve deliberate parallels, stable terminology, polite register, and procedural templates.

Do not add noun endings, questions, quotes, tangents, or synonyms just to break a pattern. Minutes and slides may naturally use fragments.

## 7. Match lists and prose to content

Use lists for independent items, ordered steps for procedures, tables for comparison, and prose for relationships requiring explanation. Do not turn a list of observations into a causal sequence without evidence. Do not convert useful action lists or advice lists into paragraphs merely to vary appearance.

## 8. Check terminology and references

Identify unfamiliar terms at their first useful occurrence. Explain them for this reader; keep exact identifiers and quoted labels. Check ambiguous 「これ」「それ」「この」 references and replace them only with a supported referent.

Keep spelling consistent where inconsistency could imply different concepts. Do not alter quotes, UI labels, source titles, or distinct technical terms merely to standardize appearance.

## 9. Check evidence, scope, and genre

Is a passage reporting evidence, making an inference, proposing an action, or expressing an opinion? Are the boundaries clear? Does the document type need a method, record limitation, comparison basis, or requested decision?

General definitions need not contain numbers or proper names. If a substantive claim lacks essential evidence, seek the missing material or state the limit; do not fill it with plausible detail. Do not invent the author's tentative stance to make a memo more decisive.

## 10. Read in context and decide when to stop

Read the changed passages with their neighbors, then review the document as a whole. Reading aloud can expose awkward attachment or register, but conversational sound is not the sole test for a technical specification or formal record.

Record consequential choices as changed, kept with a reason, or requiring information. Apply `eval-rubric.md` qualitatively when useful. Stop when the requested task is satisfied, meaning is preserved, substantive concerns are addressed or explained, and revisions introduce no meaningful regressions. There is no score threshold or obligation to eliminate every pattern.

## Reporting limits

If tools were not run, do not imply that they were. If source facts, reader testing, or external evidence were not checked, distinguish an editorial judgment from verification. Diagnosis does not establish whether a text was written by AI; see `diagnose.md`.
