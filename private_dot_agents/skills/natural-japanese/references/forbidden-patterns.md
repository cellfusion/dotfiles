# Expressions to review in context

The filename corresponds to the existing reference path and the lint category `forbidden_phrase`. **These expressions are not forbidden.** Most are grammatical, ordinary Japanese. A match invites a contextual review; it does not show poor writing, AI authorship, or a required replacement.

[Sources](sources.md) distinguish official guidance from this catalog's editorial heuristics. The 2022 public-writing guidance supports avoiding needless repetition and excessive lexical restrictions; it does not ban the words below. Preserve names, quotations, uncertainty, politeness, logical scope, and genre. Do not cite unavailable corpus percentages or theories about model training as evidence that a phrase is wrong.

## Conclusions and summaries

Examples: 「と言えるでしょう」「と言えるだろう」「と言えます」「ということになるでしょう」「のではないでしょうか」「結論から言うと」「結論として」「まとめると」「総じて」「いかがでしたか」「いかがでしょうか」.

Review whether the phrase adds a needed inference, summary, question, or level of politeness. Remove only an empty wrapper or unsupported claim. A tentative inference cannot become a categorical statement just because the ending is longer.

- Before: 「申請期限は7月18日ということになります。」
- Possible after: 「申請期限は7月18日です。」
- Keep: 「この結果だけでは、原因を特定できないと言えるでしょう。」 if the interpretive stance and courteous register matter.

## Emphasis and evaluation

Examples: 「非常に重要」「極めて重要」「重要なのは」「大切なのは」「ポイントは」「言うまでもなく」「言うまでもありません」「まさしく」「まさに」.

Ask what deserves attention and why. An unexplained superlative may be less useful than the actual consequence, but do not invent one. 「重要なのは」 can efficiently identify a real priority. Do not remove urgency from a warning or an evaluation from an author's stated opinion.

## Introductions and transitions

Examples: 「さて」「それでは」「このように」「このような中」「ここで注目したいのは」「見ていきましょう」「紹介していきます」「解説していきます」「深掘りしていきます」.

Remove announcements that only delay the promised answer. Keep transitions that help readers follow a change of topic, workshop sequence, or spoken presentation. A scope statement may prevent readers from expecting the wrong content.

- Before: 「ここでは申請方法を紹介します。フォームから申請してください。」
- After: 「フォームから申請してください。」

Invariant: the same request and route. Do not add a form URL, deadline, or procedure not in the source.

## Caution, limitations, and attribution

Examples: 「一概には言えません」「個人差がありますが」「あくまで一例ですが」「という側面もあります」「とも限りません」「かもしれません」「と言われています」「という声もあります」.

These can carry essential uncertainty, exceptions, or attribution. Preserve them when meaningful. If the condition or source is known, make it explicit; if unknown, do not invent it. Removing a hedge is not automatically clearer or more honest.

- Keep: 「個人差があります」 when the source intentionally limits generalization.
- Review: 「と言われています」 when the reader needs to know who says it; flag the missing attribution rather than converting it into fact.

## Conversational emphasis and empathy

Examples: 「なんですよね」「んです」「驚くべきことに」「興味深いことに」「実は」「いかがでしょう、あなたも」.

Check whether the tone fits the author's actual stance and relationship with readers. Preserve conversational voice in an essay or email. Do not add surprise, shared experience, imagined reader frustration, or first-person memories to make neutral prose warmer.

## Counts and numbered framing

Examples: 「大きく分けて3つの理由があります」「理由は3つあります」「軸は2つあります」「ポイントは主に以下の通りです」.

A count can help orientation when it matches the real items. Do not round the content into a preferred number, add padding, or ban counted lists. Procedures need ordering; a comparison may need parallel axes.

## Contrast structures

Examples: 「ではなく」「だけでなく」「というより」.

Check what is excluded, corrected, or expanded. Repetition alone does not justify deleting the distinctions. Preserve the excluded alternative even if changing the form.

- Before: 「担当は田中ではなく、佐藤です。」
- After: 「担当は佐藤です。田中ではありません。」

The second form is not necessarily better; both illustrate the same meaning. There is no document-wide count or percentage that requires a change.

## Noun endings and list fragments

Examples: 「確認済み」「対応予定」「次回検討」 and noun-ending prose.

Record fragments and headings are often useful. Add predicates only where readers cannot understand the status, actor, or relationship. Do not impose a rate of noun endings or add them to produce stylistic variety.

## Repeated structure and vocabulary

Examples: paragraphs beginning with the same connector; repeated 「重要」「様々」「多様」「大きな」; identical sentence openings.

Review whether the repetition obscures distinct content. Parallel structure may aid comparison, and repeated technical names may be essential. Replace a vague word with supported detail only when readers need it; avoid synonym rotation for the same concept. Consistent public notices and reference entries should not be deliberately made uneven.

## Posture without substance

Examples: 「正面から扱う」「正面から見る」「正面から書く」「正面から立てる」「正面から回収する」.

If the phrase merely claims thoroughness, state the actual subject or work instead. Keep it when the author's point really concerns their approach or an explicit contrast with prior avoidance.

- Before: 「この章では障害原因を正面から扱う。」
- Possible after: 「この章では障害原因を扱う。」

Do not add a resolved cause when the source only promises to examine it.

## Broad adjectives

Examples: 「不可欠」「核心的」「鍵となる」「根本的な」「多角的」「包括的」「総合的」.

Ask which condition, viewpoint, or necessary dependency the word expresses. A precise technical term or product name must stay intact. 「不可欠」 can express a genuine requirement; replacing it with 「役立つ」 weakens the claim. If the claim is unsupported, flag it rather than silently changing the author's degree of certainty.

## Broad verbs and announcements

Examples: 「掘り下げる」「深掘りする」「言語化する」「について見ていく」「を探求する」.

These are legitimate verbs. If a sentence only announces content already evident from a heading, it may be removable. Keep the wording when it describes the actual task or intent. Do not fabricate the result of an announced investigation.

## Dramatic endings

Examples: 「ところまでが仕事です」「に他なりません」「これこそが真髄です」「と言っても過言ではありません」.

Review whether the rhetorical emphasis fits the genre and the author's stance. Simplify only if the same claim and force survive. A strong assertion must not be weakened into a personal hunch merely to sound less dramatic.

## Padding and restatement

Examples: 「近年、重要性がますます高まっています」「ここでは詳しく見ていきましょう」「つまり、ということでもあります」.

Ask whether the passage supplies necessary background, a new implication, or useful reinforcement. Delete only what adds neither information nor reader support. An explanation for beginners may legitimately repeat a concept in different words.

## User-specific preferences

If a user dislikes a particular phrase, record the exact combination and context in a requested style profile, with the user's comment as evidence. Do not generalize it into a ban for other users, genres, or all uses of a word. See `../assets/style-profile-template.md`.

## Decision rule

Before changing a match, answer: What does the wording mean here? What reading problem does it cause? What must the alternative preserve? If no concrete problem can be identified, keep it. See `translationese.md` for construction-level alternatives and `revision-guide.md` for recording decisions.
