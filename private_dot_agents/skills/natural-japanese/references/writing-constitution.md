# Drafting principles

These twelve principles guide choices; they are not a universal style constitution or lexical ban. Use them with the relevant `doctypes/` reference and `genre-notes.md`. [sources.md](sources.md) records primary-source support and its limits. The practical adaptations and examples below are editorial heuristics.

**Preservation comes first.** In a rewrite, retain meaning, facts, agency, attribution, modality, scope, and genre. During new drafting, distinguish supplied facts from researched facts and proposed language. Do not manufacture concrete detail or an author's position to make prose more persuasive.

## 1. Give readers the information they came for

In a decision-oriented document, present the supported main finding or request early, followed by reasons and detail. Remove announcements that merely promise an explanation. A brief scope statement may be necessary; narrative writing need not reveal its conclusion immediately.

- Before: 「本稿では、申請期限について説明します。申請期限は7月18日です。」
- After: 「申請期限は7月18日です。」

Invariant: the same deadline; no new requirement or penalty. An introduction that only states a topic cannot be rewritten into a new substantive finding without evidence.

## 2. Make headings useful for navigation

A heading should identify what readers will find. A specific label, question, task, or supported conclusion can all work. Do not convert every heading to the same rhetorical form, but do not vary stable headings simply for novelty either.

- 「利用方法」 → 「領収書をアップロードして申請する」 when that is the section's actual procedure.
- Keep 「決定事項」 in minutes when it is the recognized place to find decisions.
- Keep 「導入時期は未定」 rather than upgrading it to 「来月導入する」.

There is no quota of headings to change. Preserve the body's certainty and scope in the heading.

## 3. Match prose, lists, and tables to the relationships

Use prose when readers need an explanation of causality, reasoning, or chronology. Use lists for independently retrievable points and tables for comparable attributes. Number procedures when order matters.

- Before: 「在庫が減ったため、欠品した。」
- After: 「在庫が減り、そのため欠品した。」

This preserves an explicitly supplied causal claim. By contrast, a list containing 「在庫が減った」「欠品した」 does not alone prove the cause; do not add 「そのため」 unless the source supports it. Discussion notes and advice lists can stay as bullets when that helps retrieval.

## 4. Introduce terms for the actual reader

Explain unfamiliar terms at their first useful occurrence. Choose term-first, function-first, a short parenthetical explanation, or a glossary according to the task. An expert reference may need the precise term before the explanation; a beginner tutorial may benefit from the reverse.

- Term-first: 「CQRSは、データの変更処理と取得処理を分ける設計です。」
- Function-first: 「データの変更処理と取得処理を分ける設計を、CQRSと呼びます。」

Both are illustrative definitions. Do not add a product feature or technical guarantee when only its name is supplied. Keep UI labels, API identifiers, and legally defined terms exact.

## 5. Supply evidence where a claim needs it

Use relevant facts, examples, dates, and numbers when they support the point. An abstract definition or general instruction is not defective merely because it lacks a proper noun or statistic.

Do not replace 「担当者」 with an invented person's name or 「一部」 with an invented percentage. Preserve privacy-related anonymization. If missing evidence blocks a claim, narrow the claim to what is supported or identify the missing information outside the polished passage. See `revision-guide.md`.

## 6. Make emphasis serve retrieval

Use emphasis where readers need to notice a warning, deadline, key term, or distinction. Excessive decoration can obscure priority, but there is no fixed allowance of bold phrases or symbols. Follow the document's conventions and medium. Do not change formatting solely to reduce a detector count.

## 7. Allocate detail by purpose, not by artificial unevenness

Give a consequential or complex point enough explanation. A simple point may need only a sentence. Equally important items may deserve equal treatment, especially in comparison tables, procedures, and recurring records.

Do not invent a ranking, stage plan, or dismissive assessment just to create contrast. Do not add a personal anecdote, feeling, or “temperature.” The amount of explanation should follow the evidence, reader's knowledge, and task—not a target distribution of paragraph lengths.

## 8. Review repetition for its function

Repeated wording can aid safety, reference lookup, comparison, and deliberate rhetoric. Repeated explanations can also waste time. Ask what the repetition does before changing it.

Do not force a third occurrence into a question, quote, or noun ending. Do not alternate short and long sentences merely to change rhythm. Keep technical terms stable; synonym rotation can make readers think one concept has several meanings.

## 9. Use contrasts when they express a real distinction

「～ではなく」 and 「～だけでなく」 can express exclusions, corrections, scope, or emphasis. Keep contrasts that carry those meanings. If multiple phrases merely restate the same distinction, simplify without losing the excluded alternative.

- Before: 「対象は社員ではなく、契約社員です。」
- Possible after: 「対象は契約社員です。社員は対象外です。」

Invariant: both inclusion and exclusion. Changing this to 「対象は契約社員です」 alone can lose the explicit exclusion. There is no allowable percentage of contrast constructions.

## 10. Preserve certainty and modality

Differentiate confirmed facts, estimates, hypotheses, plans, proposals, permissions, and obligations. Use natural wording such as 「予定」「未定」「可能性がある」 or a visible label when that helps. Do not require a label on every sentence or strip meaningful hedges.

- 「来月導入する予定です」 must not become 「来月導入します」.
- 「増える可能性があります」 must not become 「増えます」.
- 「検討してください」 must not become 「実施してください」.

Disclose record limitations and corrections when relevant to the reader. Do not invent a correction history, recording method, or verification claim.

## 11. Distinguish evidence from interpretation

Attribute quotations and claims to the right speaker or source. Make the difference between results and interpretation understandable without adding 「筆者は」 mechanically to every opinion.

A source assertion unsupported by the supplied material is not repaired by turning it into an invented first-person belief. Preserve the author's stance when it exists; otherwise request evidence or flag the concern separately. New research should cite sources for claims requiring support.

## 12. End where the document's purpose is fulfilled

A decision memo may need a requested decision. A guide may finish with the last step and troubleshooting. Minutes may finish with unresolved items. An essay may leave space for reflection. A descriptive report can stop at findings and limitations.

Add implications or recommendations only when requested or appropriate and supported. Do not manufacture a universal lesson, next action, or commitment just to avoid a conventional ending. A short summary can be useful; repetition is not always waste.

## Structural read-through

Read headings and paragraph openings together. Can readers locate the main answer, follow the logic, and tell what is evidence or still unresolved? Then read the full text: the outline alone cannot establish fidelity, readability, or the adequacy of supporting evidence.

## Sentence-level review

See `readability-principles.md` and `readability-antipatterns.md`. Clarify subject–predicate relations, modifier attachment, reference words, logical connections, and punctuation. The 2022 public-writing commentary's 50–60-character cue is a prompt to inspect readability, not a length limit. Shorten only when the meaning and function survive.

When principles compete, first preserve the source, then choose what benefits the stated reader. Do not treat a tool finding or stylistic preference as stronger authority than the user's purpose.
