# Research memos, discussion papers, and proposals

## Purpose

A memo can show what is known, what remains open, and what readers are being asked to consider. A proposal can advocate an option; an exploratory memo may have no preferred option yet. Do not manufacture an author's position to make an open investigation look decisive.

**Source guidance:** The [2022 public-writing recommendation](../sources.md) supports clear purpose, early main points, evidence, and audience-aware structure. The memo patterns below are editorial heuristics, not a requirement for every memo to have a central assertion or tentative preference.

## Information to consider

- The question, proposal, or requested decision.
- Purpose, scope, and relevant exclusions.
- Background needed to understand the issue.
- Available evidence, with its source and limits.
- Options and meaningful common comparison criteria, when available.
- Open questions and what would be needed to answer them.
- The author's actual stance, if supplied.

The opening should make the memo's purpose clear. If the conclusion is tentative, preserve that status. If no conclusion exists, open with the question or reason for investigation instead of an invented recommendation.

## Organization

A useful order is purpose or tentative finding, relevant background, evidence or options, unresolved issues, and the requested next discussion. Adapt the order to the material; a short research note may not need all these sections.

Use tables for comparable options when criteria and values are actually available. Do not invent trade-offs, costs, priorities, or a preferred design to fill a table. Missing values should remain unknown or be addressed with focused research when the task authorizes it.

Define unfamiliar terms inline or in a glossary according to the reader's needs. Stable definitions can be easier to use than varied phrasing. Do not convert parallel questions into a staged implementation plan unless the source supplies that sequence.

## Evidence and authorial agency

Separate established facts, reported claims, hypotheses, and proposals. Preserve uncertainty and source attribution. A supplied personal observation can support a memo; one must not be invented merely to increase specificity or “warmth.” An abstract framework can be appropriate if that is the task.

Do not add 「自分はA案に傾いている」 when the author only lists questions. Do not convert 「検討が必要」 into a commitment to build or fund something. A memo can remain useful as a structured set of open questions.

## Before/after: organize questions without adding a position

**Before**

> # 地域防災アプリの検討について
>
> 近年、災害時の情報伝達手段としてスマートフォンアプリが注目されている。本メモでは地域防災アプリの可能性について検討する。
>
> 検討すべき論点は以下の通りである。
> - 通知の仕組みをどうするか
> - 高齢者にも使いやすいUIをどう実現するか
> - 平時の利用をどう促すか
>
> これらについては今後さらなる検討が必要である。

**After**

> # 地域防災アプリ：通知・使いやすさ・平時利用の検討
>
> 地域防災アプリの可能性を検討する。災害時の情報伝達手段としてスマートフォンアプリが注目される中、次の論点を整理する必要がある。
>
> - 通知の仕組みをどうするか
> - 高齢者にも使いやすいUIをどう実現するか
> - 平時の利用をどう促すか
>
> これらの論点は、さらに検討する必要がある。

**Preservation invariants:** the same topic, stated background, three open questions, exploratory purpose, and need for further consideration. Necessity is not a commitment to act. This editing example does not verify the general background claim.

**Do not add:** a low activation rate, a claim that non-use is the largest failure factor, a hybrid push-and-siren preference, a lack of in-house testing expertise, or a proposed external research budget. Those would be new substantive material, not stylistic improvements.

## When new drafting is requested

If the user asks for a proposal rather than a faithful rewrite, develop options from supplied facts or authorized research. Label the proposed stance as a proposal and explain its basis. Do not present new analysis as something the author previously believed or experienced. Confirm missing decision constraints only when they cannot be obtained from the material.

## Genre distinctions

- **Minutes** record a meeting's actual discussion and outcomes; see `minutes.md`.
- **Reports** present findings and limitations and may include interpretation; see `report.md`.
- **Research memos** explore a question and can remain unsettled.
- **Proposals** request consideration or approval of a supported option, but are not already approved plans.

No document type makes uncertainty illegitimate. Preserve the supplied status and voice instead of forcing every piece into the same decisive tone.

## Quality review

Can readers tell what the memo asks them to consider? Are facts, hypotheses, options, and open questions distinguishable? Are comparisons based on common supported criteria? Is the author's stance preserved rather than supplied by the editor? Does the ending fit the request without inventing a decision or next action?

Use `../eval-rubric.md` qualitatively; there is no mandatory stance, personal anecdote, or score threshold.
