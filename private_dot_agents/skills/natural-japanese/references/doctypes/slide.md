# Slide outlines: make the message and evidence traceable

## Purpose

A slide outline helps an audience follow a presentation or skim a deck. The amount of text depends on whether slides accompany speech, stand alone, or serve as a reference. Do not assume a fixed viewing time or require every deck to end with a requested action.

**Source guidance:** The [2022 public-writing recommendation](../sources.md) supports useful headings, clear main points, audience awareness, and visual presentation of information. The slide structure below is an editorial heuristic, not an official rule about slide counts or bullet quotas.

## Useful elements

A slide may contain:

- A topic or task title.
- A supported takeaway or the question under discussion.
- Evidence, a diagram, a comparison, or the information needed to understand the takeaway.
- Necessary sources, limitations, and speaker notes.

The title and takeaway can be the same text. A reference table, workshop agenda, demonstration, or open-question slide need not force an assertive conclusion. Use as much supporting content as the claim needs, within the presentation's actual time and format constraints. Do not require three to five bullets or three groups.

## Deck structure

Identify the audience's purpose and the supported central message, then arrange the necessary material. A proposal may move from problem to evidence to requested decision. A research briefing may move from findings to method and limitations. A teaching deck may use questions and gradual explanation.

Respect a requested slide count or presentation duration. If the material cannot fit without omitting essential conditions or evidence, explain the trade-off rather than padding or compressing invisibly. The number of slides is not simply the number of conclusions.

Read titles and takeaway lines together to inspect the argument. Then inspect the evidence: a coherent sequence of headings does not prove the claims. Keep uncertainty, populations, units, periods, and limits visible in the message they qualify, not solely in hidden speaker notes.

## Before/after using the same qualitative evidence

**Before**

> ## タイトル: 新営業支援ツールについて
> ### メッセージライン: 新ツールの導入メリット
> - 業務効率が向上する
> - 情報共有がスムーズになる
> - 顧客満足度の向上が期待できる

**After**

> ## タイトル: 新営業支援ツールの導入メリット
> ### メッセージライン: 業務効率と情報共有が改善し、顧客満足度の向上も期待できる
> - 業務効率が向上する
> - 情報共有がスムーズになる
> - 顧客満足度の向上が期待できる

**Preservation invariants:** the same qualitative benefits and the same tentative customer-satisfaction claim. The edit does not make the benefits measured facts; a separate evidence review may still question them.

**Do not add:** a two-hour weekly saving, twelve minutes of duplicate entry per case, automatic CRM synchronization, a three-team pilot, or a measured 1.8-hour saving. A more specific slide needs real material, not invented evidence.

## Illustrative fictional measured result

This is a separate fictional sample, not an enriched rewrite of the before text above. Its numbers and pilot are invented solely for illustration.

> ## タイトル: 二重入力の削減を試験導入で確認
> ### メッセージライン: 試験導入した3チームでは、導入4週間後に1人あたり週平均1.8時間の作業時間削減を確認した
> - 対象は試験導入した3チーム
> - 測定時点は導入4週間後
> - 削減時間は1人あたり週平均1.8時間

The message retains the sample population, observation time, unit, and mean. Do not generalize it to all salespeople or round it into a guaranteed two-hour saving. A real deck would need the actual measurement method and source.

## Missing material

Use supplied facts and authorized research. Do not fill a deck with placeholder metrics, invented customer stories, fictional proof presented as real, or a projected benefit made to look measured. If essential information is missing, ask a focused question or identify the exact limitation. Finish supported portions without presenting an unsupported claim as a complete result.

A clearly marked unresolved question can be an appropriate slide. A guessed numerical headline with a footnote saying “unverified” is not an adequate substitute for evidence.

## Quality review

- Can the audience identify the subject and the actual claim or question?
- Are takeaway lines no stronger than their supporting material?
- Are relevant limitations visible where the claim appears?
- Do comparisons use compatible bases, units, and time periods?
- Is repeated structure useful for comparison rather than padding?
- Does the ending fulfill the presentation's purpose without inventing an action or commitment?

See `../eval-rubric.md` for qualitative checks. No bullet count, forced variation, detector score, or numeric pass threshold proves a deck is clear.
