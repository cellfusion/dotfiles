# Style profile

> Use this template only when the user requests a profile or explicitly provides one for reuse. Instructional fields are in English; Japanese examples and excerpts may remain Japanese. Fill relevant fields from user-authorized writing samples. Leave unsupported observations absent rather than inventing a preference or biography. Do not save a profile file unless requested.

## Scope and evidence

- Intended use and genre:
- Samples reviewed: title or identifier, date if supplied, and authorized source location.
- User-stated preferences and their source:
- Limits: sample size, mixed genres, edited or coauthored text, and uncertainty.

A few samples can suggest tendencies, not a fixed personality or universal voice. Do not infer identity, emotions, beliefs, or authorship from style. Separate observed patterns from explicit preferences. Avoid reproducing private sample text unnecessarily.

## Perspective and agency

- Observed first-person forms, if any: 「私」「僕」「自分」 or no explicit first person.
- Whether the writer speaks as a participant, observer, or another supported role.
- How facts, evaluations, and uncertainty are expressed, with a short authorized excerpt if useful.
- Contexts where the perspective differs by genre or audience.

Do not add first-person memories, motives, or a stance merely to imitate the profile.

## Register and endings

- Observed use of 「です・ます」, plain style, record fragments, and quoted speech.
- Typical degree of courtesy, directness, and distance from the reader.
- Deliberate uncertainty or emphasis that should survive editing.
- Context-dependent exceptions.

Record patterns descriptively; do not create quotas for noun endings, polite endings, or hedge frequency.

## Sentence and paragraph tendencies

- Typical clause complexity and sentence focus.
- Paragraph grouping, transitions, and use of lists or tables.
- Punctuation choices that affect interpretation or voice.
- Repetition that serves comparison, emphasis, or retrieval.

Do not require artificial sentence-length variation, uneven paragraphs, or a statistical match. Preserve meaning before any stylistic tendency.

## Vocabulary and terminology

- Recurring precise terms, idioms, or metaphors supported by the samples.
- Audience-specific use of technical terms and loanwords.
- Spelling or notation conventions, with exact names and quotes exempt where necessary.
- Whether examples or numbers are used, without treating their absence as a defect.

Keep defined terminology stable. Never invent a number, example, sensory detail, or experience to imitate specificity.

## Organization

- Typical opening: supported result, question, background, episode, or another pattern.
- How explanations, evidence, and open questions are arranged.
- Typical ending and when it varies.
- Stable templates that aid lookup or comparison and should not be disrupted.

## Editing summary

- The most useful supported tendencies to preserve for the current task.
- Known uncertainties or conflicts among samples.
- Explicit user instructions that override a tendency.

A profile is an aid, not an instruction to force every text into one form. Reassess its fit for the current reader and genre. Do not describe the writer's style as proof of human or AI authorship.
